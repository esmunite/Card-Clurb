What is this?

Card Clurb is a bot that hunts down underpriced Pokémon cards on eBay. Every 4 hours it scans listings for graded cards, raw singles, and sealed boxes, scores each one 0–100, and posts the 115 best deals on the homepage. That's the whole thing.

How it Works

Card Clurb watches eBay around the clock. Every 4 hours it scans dozens of tracked searches — graded singles, raw ungraded cards, sealed boxes — pulls every live listing, and scores each one 0–100. The 115 highest scores make the homepage.

The score starts with one question: what does this card actually sell for? We don't guess and we don't copy prices from other sites. We watch eBay itself — when an auction with real bids disappears, that's a confirmed sale, and we log the price. Average the recent sales for a card and you've got its true market value, straight from the source. The more sales we observe, the sharper that number gets. (New cards start with a researched estimate until we've seen enough real sales to take over.)

Once we know the market value, the deal score is a weighted mix of four things:

  • Discount vs. market (45%) — how far under the real selling price the listing sits. The biggest factor by far.
  • Auction urgency (25%) — auctions ending soon with little or no bidding usually close the cheapest.
  • Seller trust (15%) — feedback score and ratings. Sketchy sellers don't make the cut.
  • Listing quality (15%) — real photos of the actual card beat stock photos. Every time.

Each deal also gets a label so you know what kind of opportunity it is:

  • Mispriced BIN — a buy-it-now listing priced at least 15% under market. Someone listed it too low; first buyer wins.
  • Auction snipe — an auction ending soon with barely any bids. These are the ones that slip through cheap if nobody's watching.
  • Under market — priced below market but not by enough to trigger the other two. Worth a look, not a rush.

Some listings can never score as deals, no matter how cheap they look. Anything flagged as a reprint, proxy, custom, or fake gets tossed. Same for beat-up cards — heavily played, damaged, creased — hiding behind a mint-condition price. Graded listings have to name a real grading company (PSA, BGS, CGC, SGC, and the rest) or they're out too.

One last thing: every listing is re-checked to make sure it's still active before it goes on the page, so you won't click through to dead auctions.
